Why I started

E-bike and e-moped packs get thrown away while many of the cells inside are still good. Checking one cell properly takes a full capacity test, 4.45 hours on average. A lab battery cycler was far beyond my budget, so I built the instrument myself. For cells, I cold-called more than 20 bike and scooter shops and asked for their dead packs.

What I did

I built the rig from a Raspberry Pi 4B and four SkyRC MC3000 chargers: 16 bays for $692 of core hardware, plus an internal-resistance meter and a 0.01 g scale. From the donated packs I pulled about 550 used cells and registered 380. I chose what to test and the protocol, then ran 240 paired tests from 3 packs (1 VanMoof e-bike, 2 NIU e-mopeds) between 4 and 12 August 2026.

What didn't work

On packs it had never seen, my best model was off by about 101 mAh. Just predicting the average was off by 70 mAh. With only 3 packs, that is the honest result, and I report it as found. What works is the instrument, the dataset and the protocol. Next I need many more packs, from more brands.

How it was built

AI coding agents wrote much of the software, about 11,000 lines of Python, to my spec. I tested everything on the real hardware and caught their mistakes by checking the data. One agent had silently never collected a measurement it was supposed to log.

What I'm curious about

Can a fast test ever replace a slow one on cells it hasn't seen? If it can, the trained model belongs in a small dedicated device that anyone sorting used cells can plug a cell into and get an answer in seconds.
